HOW A FORTY-CENT
BRACKET COST $2.1 MILLION

For eleven months, a stamping plant blamed its welding cell. The robot faulted nine times a shift, always at a different moment, and always cleared itself before a technician arrived. Three vendors visited. None found anything.

The answer came from the logs, not the cell. When we plotted every stop against the time since the last shift change, the faults clustered at a spot where spatter had built up on a proximity sensor mount. The sensor itself was fine. Its bracket flexed by a fraction of a millimeter as it warmed.
